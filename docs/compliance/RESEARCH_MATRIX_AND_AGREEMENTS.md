# TurfCut — Multi-Sector Legal Compliance Research & Implementation Requirements
**Research snapshot:** 2026-10-23. **Not legal advice; verify counsel-reviewed source text, jurisdiction and effective date before activation.** This is a requirements matrix, not a completed nationwide legal opinion.

## Key design rule
TurfCut must differentiate (1) **sector**: political / nonprofit / private; (2) **purpose**: petition, electoral persuasion, nonpartisan civic, charitable fundraising, commercial sales, genuine research, etc.; (3) **activity**: door-to-door, phone outreach, phone survey, in-person polling, polling-place outreach; (4) **work arrangement**: remote / office / field; (5) **communications method**: live manual, automated, prerecorded, text. Different law may apply to a slightly different purpose or method.

Only counsel-approved **versioned** jurisdiction/rule policies with effective/expiry date, sources and scope should authorize publishing, shift scheduling, or call launch. Unknown must be 'needs review' rather than 'permitted'. Existing `src/lib/jobs.ts` jurisdiction hard stop remains authoritative; the proposed engine should integrate with it **after approved migration**.

## Activity-by-activity compliance matrix

| Activity | Primary legal issues | TurfCut product controls and agreements |
|---|---|---|
| Petition circulation / ballot measures | State/local petition circulator eligibility and registration, residency rules where applicable, signatures/affidavits, compensation type and pay, handling sensitive voter information, deadlines | Verified rule profile by jurisdiction and measure, required credentials, verified-only accepted signature payout, paper custody, worker issue-specific briefing and attestation |
| Candidate/issue door-to-door canvassing | First Amendment protections; applicable time/place/manner, trespass and private property rights; truthful sponsor ID and campaign notices where applicable; local rules | No universal 9–9 rule; approved locality policy; safety training, refusal handling; employer-approved job message and materials; avoid political inference |
| Nonprofit door-to-door fundraising | State charity registrations, paid solicitor/fundraising counsel licensing or disclosures where applicable, truthful charitable claims, donation custody, local solicitation rules | Sponsor verification and category; approved registrations; donor disclosure and handling, charity payment separate from wages, receipts and opt-outs; role-specific acknowledgments |
| Private door-to-door sales/lead gen | City/state peddler/solicitor permits where applicable, FTC Cooling-Off Rule and state cancellation rights if covered, consumer privacy, truthful sales representations, wage and employee classification | Permit and scope validation; cancellation disclosures/receipts and consent in approved sales integrations; no unsupported commissions; sales staff job-specific conduct agreement |
| Phone banking — political | FCC TCPA constraints for automated/prerecorded calls and SMS; consent and method matter; campaign communications may differ from commercial TSR rules; state restrictions may be stricter | Call purpose and tech classification, consent and suppression, approved state/time window per recipient, auditable stop controls, training; remote vs office |
| Phone banking — nonprofit | Third-party paid telefundraisers may fall under TSR; charity-specific DNC obligations and truthful identification; state fundraising restrictions | Determine direct charity vs third-party solicitor, disclose donor solicitation, confirmed consent and suppression, never infer eligibility from nonprofit status alone |
| Phone banking — private | FTC TSR for covered telemarketing, FCC TCPA, national/entity-specific DNC, state/local telemarketing law, automated messaging and call recording consent | Recipient time zone, 8 a.m.–9 p.m. **only as applicable TSR baseline**, state overrides, caps, opt-out, calling method and consent gate |
| Phone polling — genuine survey | Distinction from survey used for marketing (survey + sales pitch loses simple exemption); FCC autodialer/prerecorded or texts and state privacy/recording rules; respondent privacy and methodological integrity | Survey purpose disclosure, no sales cross-sell without approval; respondent data separate, worker records only verified counts, remote/office support |
| Door-to-door polling | Local access/trespass, privacy and consent, researcher identification, survey confidentiality, applicable canvassing limits | Respect refusals, approved work-area policy, field safety, interview records without storing raw answers in payroll events |
| Polling Place Outreach | State-specific electioneering buffer zones, early-vote specific policies, prohibitions on petition circulation, official observer/poll-worker distinctions, voter intimidation and obstruction | Site-specific restrictions and approved work zones with date/election profile; no assumption campaign worker is election official; hard block unknown |
| Official poll workers (future, separate) | Public election authority approval and hiring, election-officer ethics and procedures | Not eligible for ordinary candidate campaign job posting; separate official authority partnership |
| All job types | Employment/contractor classification, minimum wage/overtime, equal access, state wage rules, health and safety, privacy, child labor/age restrictions as appropriate, truthful work verification and pay | Classification review, job-specific compensation disclosures, audit/append-only pay, safety escalation, privacy minimum, no forced waivers of nonwaivable rights |

## Verified federal / national research anchors
1. **Door-to-door political/noncommercial advocacy:** Supreme Court, *Watchtower Bible & Tract Society v. Village of Stratton*, 536 U.S. 150 (2002), struck down broad municipal registration rule for advocacy. https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/536/150
2. **FTC TSR:** scope, covered charitable telefundraising, baseline permitted covered outbound calls 8 a.m.–9 p.m. in recipient's local time (without prior consent to different timing), DNC and misrepresentation: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule and https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/telemarketing-sales-rule
3. **Survey + sales:** FTC explains pure surveys and sales-pitch surveys differ for TSR/DNC: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule
4. **TCPA:** 47 U.S.C. §227 and FCC rule 47 CFR 64.1200, specific technology and recipient rules; no blanket nonprofit exception for autodialed/prerecorded calls to mobile phones. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227 ; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-64.1200 . FCC older guidance https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-12-1476A1.pdf (check subsequent precedent/revisions before relying on it).
5. **Charitable solicitation:** IRS warns roughly 40 states have charitable solicitation registration statutes and state paid solicitor requirements. https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-solicitation-initial-state-registration ; https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-solicitation-state-requirements
6. **501(c)(3) political campaign restriction:** qualifying charitable nonprofits generally cannot support/oppose candidates, while qualifying nonpartisan voter education can be permitted. https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/restriction-of-political-campaign-intervention-by-section-501c3-tax-exempt-organizations
7. **Polling-site restrictions:** NCSL 2026 cross-state electioneering review. Actual controlling laws and election-administrator guidance must be validated per site/area. https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/electioneering-prohibitions
8. **Worker classification:** DOL describes ongoing 2026 rulemaking and 2025 changes to enforcement approach, illustrating why a universal 'independent contractor' checkbox is insufficient. https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification/rulemaking ; https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/fab/fab2025-1.pdf
9. **Door-to-door consumer sales:** FTC Cooling-Off Rule and consumer cancellation rights require activity-specific review. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/cooling-period-sales-made-home-or-other-locations

## Jurisdiction research backlog — required before actual launch
- **All 50 states + DC:** source-specific registry covering charity and professional fundraisers; commercial solicitation; telemarketing statutes/calling-hour restrictions/call caps; recording consent; worker employment/pay classification; petition circulator eligibility/payout; polling place/electioneering. Don't copy unsourced marketing infographic values.
- **Cities/counties:** door-to-door hour rules, sales permits, local charitable activity, restricted public/private spaces and election authority site instructions.
- **Federal/purpose distinctions:** genuine political advocacy vs commercial marketing vs charity direct vs third-party telefundraising vs genuine surveys vs mixed sales surveys.
- Every legal entry requires: statute/regulatory URL, activity/purpose/scope, jurisdiction, exemption and edge cases, effective/expiry, source verification date, reviewer identity, approval event, expected product behavior, regression test and owner of the policy.
- Establish source monitoring and review intervals; legal requirements change. No automated updating of legal rules from unaudited web text.

## Suggested system architecture (pending DB approval)
**RuleProfile**: id, version, legal jurisdiction hierarchy and timezone, applicable sector/purpose/activity/communicationMethod, source URLs, verifiedOn, effectiveFrom, expiresAt, approvedBy, approvedAt, clauses[], required disclosures/permits, opening window/holiday restrictions if verified, consent and suppression predicates. **JobRuleSnapshot** pinned to active version when approved; **WorkerAgreementTemplate** id/version/locale/scope/text or hash; **WorkerAcceptanceEvent** append-only: workerId, jobId nullable for signup, templateVersion, signedAt, identity auth reference, clear affirmative action and copy delivered, evidence metadata (minimized). Need approval before schema and legal signature rollout.
**Do not store an 'I accept' checkbox without a durable audit record then claim a binding signed agreement.** Prefer server-authenticated signature flows, separately versioned universal platform terms vs sector-specific safety rules vs specific job terms. Provide downloadable agreement copy and re-sign material changes.

## Signature / acceptance UX designs (text requires qualified attorney approval)
**Signup:** a concise Terms of Service checkbox (separate from Privacy Policy acknowledgment), link to full accessible version; worker identity/auth + timestamp + exact version + content hash in append-only record after consent. Distinguish platform use from entering an employment agreement. Show optional political preferences on a **separate** consent surface, never forced.
**Job application / acceptance:** display employer legal identity, sector/activity, pay and conditions, job-specific source-backed safety/legality instructions, materials/data handling, site/calling hours when approved. Require separate user action 'I have read and agree to the job terms and field conduct requirements'; save exact version and snapshot, optional e-sign depending on legal need and consent requirements; no hidden prechecked boxes.
**Each shift or when rules change:** brief worker acknowledgment for meaningful modified jurisdiction/role requirements, emergency and safety contacts, phone consent/dialer restrictions. User can decline and seek support; preserve audit history, avoid retroactive compensation waivers.

## Agreement draft topics for counsel (not final legal terms)
- Worker will truthfully represent their identity, sponsor, purpose and permitted role.
- Worker will observe confirmed, activity-applicable site/calling-time rules, respect refusals and property restrictions, not intimidate or mislead.
- Worker will not misrepresent that they are an election official, official survey officer, charity agent or merchant when not true.
- Worker will treat donor/respondent/voter personal information confidentially; will use approved systems rather than personal devices for payment credentials.
- Worker will document accurate hours, shifts, signatures, contacts, interviews and accepted units; never fabricate or duplicate.
- Worker knows how to pause work, report safety incidents, ask for compliance review and dispute incorrect pay.
- Platform and employer shall disclose actual compensation and applicable employment classification; agreement doesn't waive legal wage rights or establish contractor status by declaration alone.
- For phone work: obey verified outreach scripts, opt-outs, method-specific consent and calling-hour requirements; no calls when platform displays a legal hold.
- For nonprofit work: do not promise tax deductibility without charity documentation; never route donor donations to worker payment accounts.
- For sales: provide verified mandated notices/cancellation documents and never misrepresent products or terms.
- For polling place outreach: follow site-specific restricted zones and avoid influencing or obstructing voters where prohibited.

## Incremental delivery plan
- **C0 now:** research matrix, versioned agreement templates under legal review, pure fail-closed eligibility validator and tests. Not wired to prod enforcement.
- **C1 after legal, schema and UX approval:** implement approval workflow, immutable agreement records, visible worker consent screens, organization rule review and testing.
- **C2 after state coverage and source approval:** wire approved activity profile into publish/schedule/action gates. Preserve and extend existing jurisdiction hard stop, never make a client-only enforcement claim.
- **C3:** telephony and donation/payments integrations only under separate contracts, privacy/security review and operator approval.
